Lesson 01 · Design Fundamentals & Mental Models

Design Thinking, Affordances & Signifiers

Design is not visual decoration; it is how things work. Learn the concepts of affordances, signifiers, and user mental models.

Time ~10 min Win Apply Don Norman's principles of affordances, signifiers, and mental models to eliminate user confusion Mission link Build intuitive software that users understand immediately without training manuals

1The myth of 'User Error'

Have you ever walked up to a door, pulled on a brass handle, and slammed your face into the glass because it was a push door? That is a Norman door (named after cognitive scientist Don Norman). You did not fail: the designer failed by providing misleading visual signals.

When users struggle with software, engineers often blame "stupid users". In truth, user confusion is almost always the result of a broken mental model. Users form expectations based on real-world mechanics and existing digital products. Good UI aligns with those mental models.

Key idea When users make mistakes, the interface provided conflicting signifiers or violated user mental models.

2Signifiers in digital interfaces

In software, glass screens have no physical affordances except touching glass. Everything depends on signifiers:

  • A button needs elevation, distinct background color, and borders to signify clickability.
  • Text inputs need a recessed box, blinking cursor, and clear outline to signify text entry.
  • Sliders need a groove and circular thumb handle to signify sliding.

When design trends (like aggressive ultra-flat design) strip away signifiers, users waste time clicking plain text and hunting for interactive controls.
