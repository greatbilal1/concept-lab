Visual Hierarchy, Scanning Patterns & Gestalt

Users do not read web pages word-for-word; they scan. Master the F-pattern, Z-pattern, and Gestalt grouping laws.

Time ~10 min Win Establish clear visual hierarchy using size, contrast, and Gestalt principles of proximity and similarity Mission link Guide user attention effortlessly to primary call-to-actions and key information

1How humans scan interfaces

Eye-tracking research conducted across thousands of users reveals a universal truth: users do not read web pages; they scan.

Scanning follows predictable geometric patterns:

  • F-Pattern: Dominates text-heavy pages (articles, documentation). Users scan horizontally across the first heading, down the left edge, read a shorter second line, and scan down.
  • Z-Pattern: Dominates landing pages and marketing banners. The eye scans top-left (logo), top-right (nav/login), diagonally down to bottom-left (hero text), and across to bottom-right (primary Call-To-Action button).
Key idea Design for scanning: position critical value propositions and call-to-actions along natural F and Z scan lines.

2The Gestalt Principles of visual grouping

The human brain is a pattern-matching machine. Gestalt psychology defines how we visually organize information:

  • Proximity: Items placed close together form a group. A label must be closer to its input field than to the preceding input!
  • Similarity: Elements that look alike (same color, shape, font) are perceived as having the same function (e.g. all primary buttons are blue).
  • Common Region: Enclosing items inside a card or bordered box groups them together instantly.
