Lesson 06 · Encryption & Certificates

Certificates and Certificate Authorities

How do you know you are really talking to your bank and not an impostor on the coffee shop Wi-Fi? Learn about the X.509 chain of trust and Let's Encrypt.

Time ~10 min Win Explain X.509 certificate chains, Certificate Authorities, and automated ACME issuance Mission link Prevents Man-in-the-Middle attacks and validates digital identity

1The Man-in-the-Middle problem

Encryption alone is not enough. If you connect to bank.com, an attacker on your local network could impersonate the bank, generate their own public key, and encrypt the connection with you while forwarding traffic to the bank. This is a Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) attack.

To prevent impersonation, the internet relies on Digital Certificates issued by trusted Certificate Authorities (CAs). A certificate binds a domain name to a specific public key, backed by the cryptographic digital signature of a CA.

Key idea A certificate is a cryptographically signed identity card proving that a public key belongs to a domain.

2The certificate chain of trust

Every certificate is validated upwards through intermediate certificates to a Root CA pre-installed in your OS.

3Tracing ACME automated issuance

Trace how Let's Encrypt issues free certificates in seconds using automated HTTP-01 challenges.
