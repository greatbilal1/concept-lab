TTL, caching, and propagation

Why does changing a DNS record take minutes or hours to take effect? Master Time to Live (TTL), resolver caching, and how to plan zero-downtime migrations.

Time ~10 min Win Calculate DNS cache expiration and plan zero-downtime DNS migrations using TTL Mission link Prevents downtime and broken deployments during server transitions

1The economics of DNS caching

If every browser query hit authoritative servers directly, the internet's DNS infrastructure would collapse under billions of queries per second. DNS works because almost every answer is aggressively cached.

Every record carries a TTL (Time to Live) in seconds. A resolver caches the answer for that duration. During that window, any changes you make to your records on your authoritative nameserver are completely invisible to cached clients.

Key idea A DNS record update will not be visible to cached clients until its previous TTL expires.
