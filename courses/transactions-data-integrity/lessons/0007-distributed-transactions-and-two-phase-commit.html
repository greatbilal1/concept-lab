Distributed transactions and two-phase commit

What happens when a transaction spans three different microservices with three different databases? Discover Two-Phase Commit (2PC) and why distributed transactions are hard.

Time ~10 min Win Explain the two phases of Two-Phase Commit (2PC) and its operational vulnerabilities Mission link Bridges single-node database ACID guarantees and distributed microservices

1When one database isn't enough

Single-node databases give you ACID guarantees for free. But in modern distributed architectures, an order might involve deducting inventory in the Inventory Service, charging a card in the Payment Service, and creating an order in the Order Service.

How do you ensure all three databases commit or abort atomically? The classical distributed protocol is Two-Phase Commit (2PC): a coordinator manages a Prepare Phase (asking all nodes 'Can you commit?') followed by a Commit Phase (commanding 'Commit now!').

Key idea Two-Phase Commit coordinates atomic all-or-nothing commits across multiple independent databases.
