1Core principles of Chain-of-Thought (CoT): 'Think Step by Step'

In 2022, Jason Wei and researchers at Google Brain published a monumental paper: 'Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models'. They revealed that asking a model for a direct answer to a complex problem causes it to fail, but asking it to think step by step unlocks stunning reasoning capability.

Key idea Unlocking multi-step reasoning: Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Wei et al. (2022), and why intermediate tokens solve logic errors.

2Direct Answering vs Chain of Thought

Why does Chain-of-Thought (CoT) work? Because of the physics of autoregressive transformers:

3Tokens as Computational Scratchpad

  • No Computation Without Tokens: A transformer does not have a hidden pause button where it computes for 10 seconds before emitting token #1. Its 'thinking' happens strictly as it generates tokens!
  • Autoregressive Conditioning: Every token emitted becomes part of the prompt for the next token. When a model writes out Step 1 and Step 2, Step 3 is conditioned on those established deductions!
  • Eliminating Premature Guessing: If forced to output the final answer immediately in token 1, the model has only one forward pass to guess the solution to a complex 10-step math problem.
# Standard Prompt (Fails on multi-step math):
Q: Roger has 5 tennis balls. He buys 2 cans of tennis balls. Each can has 3 balls.
   How many tennis balls does he have now?
A: 10 (WRONG! Model guessed without computing intermediate steps!)

# Chain-of-Thought Prompt (Succeeds!):
Q: Roger has 5 tennis balls. He buys 2 cans of tennis balls. Each can has 3 balls.
   How many tennis balls does he have now? Think step by step.
A: 1. Roger starts with 5 balls.
   2. 2 cans of 3 balls each = 2 * 3 = 6 balls.
   3. 5 + 6 = 11 balls.
   Final Answer: 11 (CORRECT!)

The Golden Rule of Logic: If a task requires more than one step of deduction, never ask for the final answer directly. Always require the model to show its intermediate reasoning first!
