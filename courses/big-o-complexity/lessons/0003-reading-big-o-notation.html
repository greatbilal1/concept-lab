Lesson 03 · Notation

Reading Big O notation

You have been counting work and comparing growth. Now you need the shorthand everyone else uses — the notation itself. It is smaller and simpler than it looks.

Time ~10 min Win Read O(n) out loud correctly Mission link The vocabulary for comparing solutions

1What the symbols actually say

When someone writes $O(n)$, they are making a claim about a ceiling. Read it as: "the work grows no faster than proportional to $n$". The letter $O$ stands for order, and the thing in brackets is the growth shape.

The precise version is worth knowing once, because it explains why the notation is so loose. $f(n) = O(g(n))$ means there is some constant $c$ and some starting point $n_0$ such that for every $n$ past $n_0$:

$$f(n) \le c \cdot g(n)$$

In words: past some point, $f$ never exceeds a fixed multiple of $g$. That "fixed multiple" is exactly the constant factor you are allowed to ignore — and it is why $O$ is a statement about shape, not about speed.

For most day-to-day work, $O$ is the one you want: it is the honest worst-case ceiling, and it is what "this algorithm is $O(n \log n)$" means when a colleague says it.

Key idea $O$ is an upper bound, $\Omega$ is a lower bound, $\Theta$ is both at once. When people say "Big O" casually, they usually mean $\Theta$ — the tight shape.

✓ Your win for today

You can read $O(n)$, $O(\log n)$ and $O(n^2)$ out loud as plain English sentences, and you know that $O$ is a ceiling rather than a stopwatch reading. That is enough to follow any technical conversation about performance.

5Do this next

  1. Write the sentence "$O(n^2)$" out in plain English, without using the word "big" or the letter O.
  2. Find one algorithm you use and look up its stated complexity. Write down which of $O$, $\Omega$ or $\Theta$ the source actually means.
  3. Explain to yourself why $O(n)$ is a true statement about a program that actually takes $3n + 7$ steps. (Hint: pick $c = 10$.)
