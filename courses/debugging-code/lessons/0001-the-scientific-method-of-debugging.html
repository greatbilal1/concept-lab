Lesson 01 · The Debugging Mindset

The scientific method of debugging

Stop guessing and start observing. Software behaves deterministically; this lesson introduces the loop of observation, hypothesis, and experiment.

Time ~10 min Win Distinguish defect, infection, and failure in any bug report Mission link The foundation of all systematic diagnostic craft

1The defect-infection-failure chain

A bug is not a single point in time. It is a chain of three distinct events. First, a human writes a defect in the code. Second, when executed with certain inputs, the defect causes an internal infection of variable state. Third, that infected state eventually surfaces as an observable failure.

When users file a bug report, they are reporting the final failure. If you try to fix the failure where it appears, you will likely mask the symptom rather than cure the infection.

Key idea A defect creates an infection in state, which later explodes as a visible failure.

2The diagnostic cycle

Rather than randomly changing lines of code, systematic engineers follow an empirical loop: observe the failure, formulate a hypothesis about what state is infected, devise an experiment to test that hypothesis, and observe the result.

3Traces of infected state

Walk through an example of a discount calculation where a defect in price normalization corrupts the running balance.

4Practice

Test your understanding of the defect chain before testing your knowledge.

✓ Your win for today

You can articulate the complete chain from source defect to state infection to final failure, keeping your investigation focused on the true root cause.

5Do this next

  1. Take a recent bug in your code and identify where the defect became an infection.
  2. Write down the three stages on paper before touching the editor on your next issue.
  3. Explain to a colleague why treating the failure location rarely fixes the bug.
Primary source Read Chapter 1 of *Why Programs Fail* by Andreas Zeller for a rigorous mathematical treatment of state infection.
