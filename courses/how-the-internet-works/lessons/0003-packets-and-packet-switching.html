Lesson 03 · Packets & Routing

Packets and packet switching

The internet doesn't send files; it chops files into thousands of bite-sized envelopes called packets. Learn how packet switching revolutionized communications.

Time ~10 min Win Explain packet encapsulation, Maximum Transmission Unit (MTU), and fragmentation Mission link Explains how shared physical wires multiplex millions of concurrent streams

1Circuit switching versus packet switching

Old telephone networks used circuit switching: when you made a call, physical copper switches locked open a dedicated electrical wire between both phones for the entire duration. If nobody spoke, the wire sat completely idle and wasted.

The internet uses packet switching: your computer cuts data into small chunks (usually up to 1,500 bytes), prepends a header with source and destination IP addresses, and flings them onto the network. Millions of packets from different users share the same physical cable simultaneously.

Key idea Packet switching chops data into independent chunks that share physical wires efficiently.

2Anatomy of an IP packet

An IP packet consists of a structured binary header containing metadata, followed by the payload bytes.
