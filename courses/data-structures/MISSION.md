# Mission — Data Structures

## Why this course exists

Every program stores things: a list of users, a queue of jobs, a lookup table of
settings. Beginners reach for one tool — the list — for all of them, and then
wonder why some operations feel slow or awkward. This course exists to make the
choice deliberate. Once you can see data as *arranged* rather than just *stored*,
you start picking the structure that fits the job instead of forcing the job into
the structure you happen to know.

## What the learner can do at the end

- Choose between an array/list, a stack, a queue, a hash table and a tree for a
  given task, and say *why* that choice is right.
- Implement each of those structures from scratch in Python, and use the built-in
  version when one exists.
- Reason about the cost of an operation — "this is fast, that is slow" — in terms
  of how the data is laid out, not by memorising a table.
- Recognise the shape of a problem (last-in-first-out, first-in-first-out,
  key-lookup, hierarchy) and reach for the matching structure.

## What this course is NOT

- Not a Big-O course. Complexity is *used* here to explain layout, but the formal
  treatment lives in the Big-O & Complexity course.
- Not an algorithms course. Sorting and searching are referenced, not taught.
- Not language-specific. Examples are Python, but the ideas are general.

## Success looks like

The learner stops asking "how do I store this?" and starts asking "what do I need
to *do* with this?" — and the answer to the second question names the structure.
