The Gang of Four and pattern taxonomy

In 1994, four authors changed software engineering forever. Discover the Gang of Four (GoF), the 23 classic design patterns, and their three fundamental categories.

Time ~10 min Win Classify design patterns into Creational, Structural, and Behavioral categories Mission link The architectural taxonomy organizing all object-oriented design patterns

1A shared architectural vocabulary

In 1994, Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson, and John Vlissides (the Gang of Four / GoF) published Design Patterns. They noticed that experienced software engineers repeatedly solved identical design problems using identical structural shapes.

By giving these solutions names (Factory, Observer, Strategy), they created a shared vocabulary: saying 'let's use an Adapter here' communicates twenty lines of architectural intent in a single sentence. The 23 patterns fall into three categories: Creational (how objects are created), Structural (how objects are composed), and Behavioral (how objects communicate).

Key idea Design patterns are categorized into Creational (creation), Structural (composition), and Behavioral (communication).
