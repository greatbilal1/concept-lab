Lesson 08 · Pattern Overuse & Real-World Craft

Patternitis and knowing when not to use patterns

The disease of over-design. Learn to recognize 'Patternitis', why junior developers over-complicate simple code, and how to design for simplicity and clarity.

Time ~10 min Win Diagnose and prevent pattern overuse by applying simplicity and YAGNI principles Mission link Cultivates mature engineering judgment over intellectual showing off

1The disease of over-design

Every developer who reads the Gang of Four book catches a temporary disease known as Patternitis. Suddenly, every simple three-line function becomes an AbstractFactoryStrategyVisitorObserverAdapter! A simple shopping cart is buried under 15 layers of indirection.

Design patterns are solutions to specific, proven problems. If the problem does not exist, the pattern adds pure architectural debt and cognitive overhead. The mark of a true senior engineer is not how many patterns they can pack into a codebase, but how simple they can keep the code while still solving the business problem.

Key idea Design patterns are solutions to specific problems; applying them without the problem is over-engineering.

2The simplicity hierarchy

Choose the simplest solution that solves the current business problem cleanly.

3Tracing the cure for Patternitis

Trace how replacing an over-engineered Factory with a simple dictionary lookup deletes 80 lines of code.

4Practice

Test your memory of design pattern discipline.

✓ Your win for today

You can balance design pattern knowledge with radical simplicity, choosing the right tool for the job without over-engineering.

5Do this next

  1. Audit a complex class in your codebase and ask: 'Could this be replaced with a simple dictionary or function?'
  2. Refactor an over-engineered factory into a clean lookup map.
  3. Embrace the philosophy: 'Make it work, make it right, make it fast' in that strict order.
